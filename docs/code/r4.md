# r4: the game image from 0x800CD798 to 0x800E847C

The fourth slice of the game image (`blob`): about 240 functions that run the cars and the race around them. Its
name map is [tools/port/names/r4.tsv](../../tools/port/names/r4.tsv); this page explains what the names stand for.
Addresses below are only there to place things in the image (the map is the one place that pairs them with names);
the names are the ones the map gives.

Written 2026-11-08 by the naming agent names-r4 (PC-only wave 1, [PCONLY.md](../port/PCONLY.md) section 3).

## 1. What this part of the game does

Most of it is the car simulation, the arcade lineage's "model" code that the N64 runs on its own thread:

- **The model thread** (`ModelThread`): once a retrace it reads the pads (`ControllerUpdate`, `ControlIRQ`) and runs
  `model_iteration`, which turns the pads into each human car's controls (`UpdateModelControls`), replays or
  records ghosts (`GhostUpdate`), and steps every car (`UpdateModel`).
- **One car's step** (`UpdateModel`): crash bookkeeping (`CheckCrash`, `FinishResurrecting`), the rubber-band time
  scale, then either the respawn flight (`UpdateResurrectingCar`) or the physics (`sym`), with the AI's driving first
  for drones (`MaxPathControls`); the dead-reckoning base for the renderer (`update_reckon_base`), the car's sounds
  (`UpdateCarSounds`) and the rumble request.
- **The physics** (`sym`): controls to forces (`ModelControls`), the drivetrain (`drivetrain`: `autoshift`,
  `engine`/`enginetorque`, `transmission`, `whatslips`), then `regular`: road contact and car-to-car collision
  (`road`, `collision`), forces (`forces1`, `forces2`), `antispin`, `torques`, `accelerations`, `velocities`,
  `positions`; and the crash test (`checkok`).
- **Respawning** (`resurrect.c`): when a car is stuck or fell off the world (`DetectDroneAbort`), where it goes
  (`Resurrect`, `FindHumanResPt`, the respawn-path tracing `TracePathPtForward`, `FindResPt`...), the flight there
  (`UpdateResurrectingCar` with `Interpolate` and quaternions), the blinking while not solid
  (`BlinkResurrectingCars`).
- **The race around the cars**: checkpoints, laps and places (`PassedCP`, `RecordLap`, `CarReportsGameOver`,
  `PlaceUnlockedCars`), the end test (`are_all_cars_stopped`), aborting a race (`AbortGame`), the drones'
  catch-up (`SetCatchup`).
- **Car sounds and the audio manager's client side**: engine, skid, road, bump sounds (`audio.c`), the listeners,
  sequences and effect commands posted to the audio thread (`audiomgr.c`).

Around that sit four front-end screens (audio options, controls, options, pause), the controller pak error dialogs
(`pakerr.c`), the player profile settings (`save.c`), the save passwords (`password.c`), the options' defaults
(`InitOptions`), the controller system (`controlsys.c`) and the game heap (`memsys.c`).

## 2. Where the original modules are

except the N64 platform code of section 5. The image is **not** one module after another: the N64 build was linked
as one whole-program unit, and the procedures come out in runs of one module, often in reverse source order, with
helpers before their callers; a static the compiler inlined everywhere leaves an 8-byte `jr ra; nop` stub where it

## 3. The main data structures

**The model record** (`tModelData`, 0x808 bytes, field for field the GameCube's; six on the N64, `pc_models` on the
PC: [CARS.md](../port/CARS.md)). The physics state of one car: the body-frame force and moment (`f`, `m`),
accelerations and velocities (`a`, `aa`, `v`, `w`), per-tyre forces and the body's corner forces, the world
position and orientation (`rwr`, `uvs`), the tyres (`tires[4]`: radius, spring, cornering stiffness, slip...), the
drivetrain (`engangvel`, `dwangvel`, `gear`, `transratio`...), the controls in (`mainin`: wheel, clutch, brake,
throttle, gear, wing, abort), the clock (`fastin`: iteration, model time, dt, `modelrun`), the respawn state
(`resurrect`: target position and orientation, start/end quaternions, `moving_state` -1 idle / -2 done), the
dead-reckoning snapshot the renderer draws (`reckon`), and flags (`crashflag`, `solid`, `hide_car`, `appearance`
bits for skids, smoke and blinking). Its car descriptor (`car`, `_tCar`: inertia, steering ratio, tyre positions,
gear and shift data) and tune (`tune`: weight, torque and transmission scales, `antispin`...) are pointers into the
game's data, u32 window addresses on the PC.

**The car record** (`tCarData`, 0x3B8 on the N64; `pc_cars`): what the rest of the game sees of a car: the
dead-reckoned position and orientation (`dr_pos`, `dr_uvs`), place and distance, the 21 visuals, the collision and
blink state (`collidable`, `solid`, `collide_time`, `collide_count`, `collide_state`), the drone's path control
(`mpctl`), `crash_state` (none, wrecked, respawn) and `dead`, the stray angle (WRONG WAY), the player pointer and the
battle state. Up to the visuals it has the GameCube's offsets; after them the GameCube's minus one visual.

**The player record** (`tPadState`, `gPlayers[4]`, 0x4C on the N64): the car slot and controller, the pad's edges
and levels, the ten control bindings, the audio listener and the profile.

**Profiles and save data** (`tPlayerProfile`, `tSaveData`): the GameCube layout less one control byte: options, the
controls, `raceType`, four per-mode settings records (track, car, transmission, view, laps...), per-track stats and
car settings, each block with its own checksum and written with `PS_WriteFile` on change.

**Paths**: `gDronePaths` (one racing line per drone path: points with a speed), `gResPath` (the respawn path with its
alternates) and `gCheckpoints` (the checkpoints, the loop and finish points, and for each checkpoint the index of its
point on every path). The tracing functions walk these with wrap-around at the loop point.

**The audio manager's client side**: commands (`tAudioMsg`) taken from a pool under `audio_lock_queue` and posted to
`audio_command_queue`; fx tags, sequences (`gFreeSequences`/`gActiveSequences`) and listeners
(`gFreeListeners`/`gActiveListeners`) are free/active linked lists; a car's sounds are sound states
(`sound`, `vol`, `pitch`, pans) in `gEngineSounds`, `gPaveSkidSounds`, `gRoadNoises` and plain handles.

**The heap** (`tHeap`, `tMemoryChunk`): first-fit chunks with 32-byte headers (magic, links, size, master, used,
lock count), a master-handle table per heap, sub-heaps chained from `gMainHeap`.

**Front-end statics**: each screen keeps MAT3's statics (`buttonInfo`, `inited`, `blit`, `gTitlePane`, scroll and
cursor speeds); the map prefixes them with the screen (`audio_`, `controls_`, `options_`, `pause_`) where MAT3
reuses the name in several files.

## 5. What is N64-only

These have no MAT3 counterpart, or only a GameCube stand-in:

- **libultra threads and queues**: `ModelThread` is an `OSSched` client (`gScheduler`) blocking on
  `model_thread_queue`; the audio manager, the heap and the serial interface are guarded by one-message
  `OSMesgQueue` locks (`audio_lock_queue`, `heap_lock_queue`, `si_lock_queue`) where MAT3 has empty
  `_GetSemaphore` functions; commands go to the audio thread through `audio_command_queue`.
- **The controllers**: `init_controllers` (`osContInit`, the SI event queue), `ControllerUpdate` (`osContStartReadData`
  and the N64 button map `n64_button_map`), the stick calibration in `ControlIRQ`, `cont_status`, `cont_bit_pattern`.
- **The Controller Pak and Rumble Pak**: the pak error dialogs and their N64 draw code (`DrawChoice3`,
  `DrawPakErrNoPak`), the rumble request in the pak records (`gPakRumbleRequest`, set by `UpdateModel`).
- **Memory**: the heap's placement after the BSS up to `osMemSize`, the Expansion Pak flag
  (`expansion_pak_present`, which doubles pools elsewhere), sub-heaps (`create_heap`, `destroy_heap`), the lock
  counts (`lock_memory`, `unlock_memory`).
- **The 8-byte stubs** of inlined statics: retail code that does nothing and is never called (section 6).

The RSP does not appear in this slice.

## 6. What could not be identified

- **Seventeen empty stubs** whose static is not known: named `stub_before_X`/`stub_after_X` after their neighbours
  (nine in the pak error code, four in the model iteration and pad code, four elsewhere). Eleven more are named
  after the static that most likely left them (`InitModelControls`, `StopStationaryEmitters`,
  `CleanupOverlay`, `audio_uninitButtons`, `audio_initButtons`, `options_uninitButtons`, `calcOptionMenuMaxSize`,
  `options_initButtons`, `SetDefaultOptions`, `no_catchup`, `multiinit`): each sits right next to the one function
  that holds that static's body inline. All are dead code a later wave can delete.
- `update_skid_emitter` (0x800E0048): the stub is probably `_UpdateSkids`'s, but the PC source gives it the body of
  one `AudUpdateEmitter` call, so the name describes that body.
- `AudPauseAllEffects` (0x800DB7B4): MAT3's pause and stop commands are both 7; the choice follows its position by
  the pause screen and the other command-7 function being `AudStopAllEffects` already.
- `DrawChoice3`: the N64's three-choice dialog for the 'recognize' error has no MAT3 counterpart (MAT3 only shows a
  message there).
- A few data are named for their use only: the screen texture names, the format strings, the N64 rodata literals
  (named after the function and the role of the constant; wave 2 can turn them back into literals).

## 7. Oddities and bugs seen

- `UpdateModel`'s solid timer tests `gRace != battle || gRace != obstacle`, always true, so every mode uses the
  long delay; MAT3 marks the same line as an original bug.
- `SetPakError` looks for a free slot among the pak's two records and, when both are busy, writes the third one
  past the row (the next pak's first record, or past the array for pak 3); MAT3 has the same loop with three.
- `GetResPt` and `FindClosestPathPt` return an uninitialised index when no point is nearer than the start distance
  (an empty path); retail does the same.
- `NewSequence` and `NewListener` take the head of their free list without checking it (the fx tags have a PC guard,
  [MORECARS.md](../port/MORECARS.md) 6; these two do not).
- `are_all_cars_stopped` answers stunt mode with the low half of `gStuntsDone`; the PC reads it from the s32 (QA.md 4).

## 8. Notes for applying the map

- **Aliases and labels.** Many names in `asm/us/*/symbols.json` in this range are not function starts but early
  guesses at round addresses inside other functions (`menu_render`, `hud_speedometer_render`, `world_load_segment`
  ...) or second names of a function. The map lists them in a comment for removal; the three aliases code still uses
  (`pause_resume`, `title_prompt_handler`, `unlock_notification`) get rows to the same new name as their function.
- **Names that exist already.** `AdjustSteer`, `AdjustSpeed` and `no_catchup` are wrong labels of functions in other
  areas today; those must move for the MAT3 names to be free. `nonmatching/game/RaceStateMachine_Update.c` defines
  its own `AudPauseAllEffects`, `AudResumeAllEffects`, `AudStopAllEffects` and `PauseGameMusic` (copies of the
  retail functions); with the map applied they clash with the real ones and should go. `include/game/*.h` (not
  included by any compiled file) already declares many of these names with stale signatures.
- **Existing data names.** `state_word_a`, `state_word_b`, `active_player_count`, `gameplay_mode`, `input_rec0` and
  `countdown_state` are MAT3's `gGameState`, `gNewGameState`, `gNumPlayers`, `gRace`, `gPlayers` and `gText`; the
  map's shared section proposes those, and maps the D_ aliases of the same addresses to them. `D_8002E8E8` maps to
  `gScheduler`, the name src/rom already gives it.
- Several MAT3 names are short and common (`sym`, `road`, `interp`, `positions`, `collision`, `stree`, `surf`):
  locals and struct members of the same spelling exist; that is legal C but worth knowing when reading.
